\section{Introduction}

National Basketball Association (NBA) teams that fall out of the playoff race often stop trying to win, because finishing worse earns a better draft pick. Roughly a third of the league's teams were openly doing this in the 2025-26 season. The league has spent ten years adjusting its lottery odds in response, most recently in May 2026 with a sixteen-team format called 3-2-1. Banchio and Munro (SSAC 2020) proved that odds alone cannot fix it, because any rule giving worse records better odds still rewards losing.

Carry-Over Lottery Allocation (\textsc{Cola}) reads something else. Each team carries a waiting number that rises every year it misses the playoffs and falls when it succeeds, and draft priority follows that number, so one bad season barely moves a team. \textsc{Cola} is a family of rules, and no version had been tested against the others. We tested them, and found the reward for losing in a part of the draft nobody argues about.

\section{Methods}

We describe the family with seven settings and run nine draft rules through ZenGM Basketball-GM, an open-source simulator that plays out every game and roster move. Each rule ran 48 separate leagues of 15 seasons, 14{,}004 league-seasons in all. Every rule answers one question asked two ways. How many places earlier does the first-ranked team draft than the fifth?

Six of the nine rules are \textsc{Cola} versions. One is new. Waitlist \textsc{Cola} keeps the four-pick televised draw exactly as the original runs it, and changes only what comes after. The original fills picks 5 through 14 in order of record; Waitlist \textsc{Cola} fills them by waiting number, longest wait first, ties broken at random.

\section{Results}

The reward for losing came mostly from those ten picks, which went in order of who lost most. Flattening the odds and leaving them alone makes tanking pay better. The league's new rule confirms it. 3-2-1 draws all sixteen places, so no pick goes by record, and that alone carries its whole effect. Its new odds would have paid tanking more than the old rule.

Several \textsc{Cola} versions remove the reward. Waitlist \textsc{Cola} does it while leaving the lottery fans watch untouched, which the steeper versions give up. Dropping a team from fifth-worst to worst gains $1.87$ places under the original \textsc{Cola} and $1.32$ under the 2026 rule, and nothing under Waitlist \textsc{Cola} across 720 seasons. The pattern repeats on 26 seasons of real NBA results.

\section{Conclusion}

Ending the reward for losing takes no new lottery. Keep the draw, order the picks behind it by waiting number, and a team gains nothing from losing while the longest-suffering teams still pick earliest, more than the rule the league just adopted delivers. Lottery night would look the same to fans. That format expires after the 2029 draft, and the seven settings map the rest of the space.
