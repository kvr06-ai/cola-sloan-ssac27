\section{Introduction}

National Basketball Association (NBA) teams that fall out of the playoff race often stop trying to win, because finishing worse earns a better draft pick. Roughly a third of the league's teams were openly doing this late in the 2025-26 season. For ten years the league has answered by adjusting its lottery odds, most recently in May 2026 with a sixteen-team format called 3-2-1. Banchio and Munro (SSAC 2020) proved that adjusting odds cannot fix this, because any rule giving worse records better odds still rewards losing.

Carry-Over Lottery Allocation (\textsc{Cola}) takes another route. Each team carries a waiting number that rises every year it misses the playoffs and falls when it succeeds, and draft priority follows that number. One bad season barely moves it. \textsc{Cola} is a family of rules, and no version has been tested against the others. We tested them, and found the reward for losing in a part of the lottery nobody argues about.

\section{Methods}

We describe the family with seven settings and test nine draft rules in ZenGM Basketball-GM, an open-source simulator that plays out every game, roster, and trade. Each rule ran 48 separate leagues of 15 seasons, 14{,}004 league-seasons in all. Every rule is then scored on one question asked two ways. How many places earlier does the team ranked first draft than the team ranked fifth? Table 2 gives both rankings.

\section{Results}

Most of the reward for losing came from picks 5 through 14, which went in order of who lost most. Flattening the odds while leaving those picks alone makes tanking pay better. The league's new rule confirms it, because 3-2-1 draws all sixteen places, so no pick is handed out by record. Its new odds table alone would have made tanking pay more than the old rule.

Table 2 shows why rules built on record are stuck. Ranked by record or by their own standard they post the same number, so they cannot help the teams they mean to help without also paying teams to lose. Waitlist \textsc{Cola} breaks that. Dropping a team from fifth-worst to worst gains $1.87$ places under the original \textsc{Cola} and $1.32$ under the 2026 rule, and nothing at all under Waitlist \textsc{Cola} across 720 seasons. The pattern repeats on 26 seasons of real NBA results.

\section{Conclusion}

Waitlist \textsc{Cola} changes one thing. Keep the four-pick lottery as it is, then hand out the remaining picks by waiting number. Losing on purpose stops paying. The league's new rule also stops it, but by pushing its worst teams further down the order, while Waitlist \textsc{Cola} gives the longest-suffering teams the earliest picks. Nothing a broadcast shows would change. The 3-2-1 format expires after the 2029 draft, when a fresh vote reopens the question.
