\section{Introduction}

For decades, the NBA has navigated the tradeoff between helping weak teams via the draft and the tanking that ensues. Carry-Over Lottery Allocation (COLA) solves the dilemma. COLA redefines weakness, using multi-season playoff results instead of a single season's record. Thus, tanking need not be rewarded. Draft priority carries over between seasons, being reduced when a team has playoff success or makes a high draft pick. Over time, more help is directed to weaker teams. 

The COLA framework allows flexibility in how draft priority changes. Its definition includes the NBA's own new 3-2-1 lottery, which has a minimal carry-over exclusion and no reward for losing.

We prove that, under reasonable assumptions, COLA can be incentive-compatible while helping the weakest team, circumventing Banchio and Munro's SSAC 2020 impossibility result for record-based lotteries. Only one assumption requires justification: teams prefer winning games over manipulating the pool of lottery teams. Others are standard assumptions.

\section{Methods}

Our incentive-compatibility proof is routine; the novelty is the reframe that makes it possible.

To discuss tradeoffs, we identify tunable dials within the COLA framework:
\begin{itemize}
\item How does a team's draft priority increase?
\item How does the priority decrease?
\item How does priority map to lottery odds and draft positions?
\end{itemize}

ZenGM Basketball-GM, an open-source simulator that plays out every game and roster move of realistic fictional leagues, lets us compare different draft mechanisms on these criteria:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Targeted help.} To what extent do the highest priority teams receive draft help?
\item \textbf{Long-term parity.} Can all teams be competitive, as measured by long playoff droughts?
\item \textbf{Simplicity.} Is the mechanism easy to explain?
\item \textbf{Incentive compatibility.} Does the mechanism reward losing? Is it still anti-tanking if the model's assumptions fail?
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

We ran nine mechanisms on the same 30 simulated leagues for 25 seasons each. We compare mechanisms by paired differences with 95\% confidence intervals. 

Simple COLA matches or beats 3-2-1 on simplicity, incentives, and targeted help. On simplicity, it orders teams by most years without a playoff series win or a top-3 draft pick, with the tiebreaker being most wins this season. No lottery is used. Unlike 3-2-1, it helps the weakest teams, where weakness is measured by drought length. Unlike the old NBA lottery, it is incentive-compatible. The improvement in help is statistically significant. On parity, 3-2-1 keeps a slight edge. Where parity matters more, we propose Full-lottery COLA, which draws all 14 lottery picks in proportion to draft priority. It matches 3-2-1 on parity while still giving more help.

\section{Conclusion}

COLA can end tanking incentives while giving the most help to the weakest teams, which record-based lotteries cannot do. The NBA's 3-2-1 lottery, an edge case of the framework, ends tanking incentives but gives almost no help to the weakest teams. Stronger carry-over delivers far more targeted help, and COLA's dials let the league trade off targeted help, parity, simplicity, and incentive compatibility.
